# Epsu Advertising And Growth Playbook 2026

As of May 24, 2026.

## Purpose

This document is not a generic marketing brainstorm. It is a practical growth plan for getting Epsu to work in the real world with a low-to-moderate budget.

The goals are:

- get a few schools to adopt Epsu
- get a few regional communities to adopt Epsu
- use affluent and well-connected adults as sponsors, introducers, and legitimacy multipliers where useful
- solve the zero-network-effect problem instead of pretending it will solve itself
- avoid expensive tactics that look impressive but do not create dense local usage

## Research Basis

This playbook was built after reviewing 200+ candidate links across:

- campus ambassador case studies
- hyperlocal and anonymous app growth histories
- network-effect and cold-start research
- referral and waitlist case studies
- school software adoption and failure research
- Estonia-specific youth, school, municipality, and student-organization sources

The synthesis below is based on patterns that repeated across those sources, not on one article.

## The Main Decision

Do not market Epsu as one product to three totally different audiences at once.

Treat them as three separate jobs:

- `Students`: create dense, funny, useful, school-specific posting behavior
- `Regional users`: create local identity, event chatter, practical local discussion
- `Affluent adults`: provide access, sponsorship, introductions, host legitimacy, or business backing

The biggest mistake would be trying to make “rich people” a primary user segment. That is the wrong wedge.

Rich people are not the first users for Epsu.
They are:

- school founders
- private-school leaders
- municipality connectors
- alumni donors
- youth-program sponsors
- local business owners
- people who can open doors, fund pilots, or lend legitimacy

If you try to sell Epsu to affluent people as a status app, you will waste time.

## The Core Growth Thesis

Epsu should not launch city-wide first.

It should launch community-by-community with enough density to feel alive inside a small boundary.

The right order is:

1. one school with strong student social density
2. one more school with a different profile
3. one regional pilot around the same geography
4. only then a second region or additional schools

This is how you beat zero network effects:

- reduce the size of the first network
- pick a group that already has repeated contact offline
- seed enough content before launch
- give users a reason to return during the first 7 days
- recruit hosts who can keep the room active without making it look fake

## What Actually Works

### 1. Dense local rollout beats broad awareness

What repeatedly works for campus and local apps is not “reach.”
It is density within a small social graph.

That means:

- one school at a time
- one municipality cluster at a time
- one student host per school
- one local host per region
- content and launch materials tailored to that exact place

Do not run Estonia-wide awareness campaigns before proving that one place can become active on its own.

### 2. Ambassadors work when they are operators, not poster machines

Campus ambassador programs can work, but only when ambassadors do real operating work:

- seed the first posts
- recruit the first 30-100 serious users
- run one or two launch events
- keep content fresh during week 1 and week 2
- feed back local issues, jokes, slang, and moderation needs

Ambassadors fail when they are paid only for downloads or vague “awareness.”

Epsu should use:

- one lead ambassador per school
- one backup ambassador per school after traction
- fixed weekly tasks
- fixed cash stipend plus milestone bonus

Not:

- unpaid “exposure”
- per-install bounties only
- giant ambassador classes with low accountability

### 3. Peer-led launch moments matter more than polished ads

For youth and campus products, the recurring pattern is:

- peer recommendation
- in-person activation
- social proof from known people
- content that looks native, not brand-made

For Epsu this means:

- student council events
- orientation weeks
- club fairs
- exam-stress weeks
- dorm or school-specific frustration moments
- local youth centre events
- municipality youth days

### 4. Referral loops help only after there is something worth referring

Referral programs are useful after initial activity exists.
They are weak when the product is empty.

Use referrals after:

- at least 30-50 weekly active users in a school Epsu
- at least 10-15 active posters/commenters
- at least some visible funny, useful, or identity-building posts

Before that, referrals mostly spread emptiness.

### 5. Safety positioning is not optional

Anonymous and pseudonymous campus apps that grew fast also repeatedly triggered:

- bullying concerns
- school administration concern
- press backlash
- police or campus safety issues
- loss of trust

For Epsu, moderation and safety are not side features.
They are part of the acquisition story.

If school leaders, youth workers, or parents think “this is just another Yik Yak,” growth gets blocked.

Your pitch has to be:

- local anonymity for users
- backend accountability
- scoped communities
- moderation tools
- report flows
- block-author controls
- temporary content lifecycle

## What Does Not Work

### 1. Broad paid ads before local density

This burns money and creates dead installs.

Do not use Meta, TikTok, or YouTube scale ads to drive people into empty communities.

Paid media should be used only for:

- validating message-market fit
- waitlist collection in a defined geography
- supporting a live pilot launch
- retargeting interested people after local traction exists

### 2. Multi-city or multi-school launches too early

This is the classic mistake.

If you launch 10 schools and 5 regions at once, you do not get 15 communities.
You get 15 empty rooms.

### 3. Ambassador programs paid only by installs

This creates:

- low-quality recruits
- shallow promotion
- fake or weak signups
- no retention accountability

Ambassadors should be measured on:

- first-week actives
- number of local posts seeded
- host introductions made
- event turnout
- retention after day 7

### 4. Generic “social app” messaging

Epsu should not sound like:

- another social network
- another school app
- another local app

It should sound like:

- your school’s anonymous drop
- your town’s local anonymous feed
- temporary, funny, honest, local posting

### 5. Enterprise school software sales motion as the main wedge

Schools adopt software slowly.
Administrators are overloaded.
Teachers often resist tools that add work.

If Epsu is sold like administrative EdTech, it will stall.

For schools, the wedge is community and student culture first.
Institutional approval matters, but it should support the student wedge, not replace it.

### 6. “Rich people” as a direct consumer segment

This is not the right first-market framing.

Affluent adults may help with:

- intros
- sponsorship
- private-school access
- alumni distribution
- local business partnerships
- event space

But the average wealthy person is not the core daily user of a youth/local anonymous app.

## The Estonia-Specific Opportunity

Estonia is small enough that concentrated rollout is realistic.

That is an advantage.

The strongest Estonia-specific channels are not mass ads. They are networks.

### Channels worth using

- Estonian School Student Councils’ Union (`Õpilasliit / ESCU`)
- Federation of Estonian Student Unions (`EÜL`) for higher-ed reach
- Teeviit and adjacent youth-information ecosystems
- Harno-adjacent youth and school networks
- municipal youth councils
- municipal youth centres
- local school student councils
- hobby schools and youth centres
- youth project and event organizers
- local student creators on TikTok and Instagram

### Why Estonia is good for Epsu

- strong digital infrastructure
- active youth-work ecosystem
- visible student representation structures
- national youth information channels
- small enough geography for city-by-city saturation
- bilingual/multilingual youth environments in some regions, which can create strong community identity if handled well

### Estonia-specific warning

Do not over-index on national press or startup-style hype.

In Estonia, the better route is:

- local proof
- named school pilots
- municipality trust
- youth-worker and student-leader buy-in
- visible safety competence

## The Best Initial Wedges

### Wedge A: State or municipal gymnasium with active student culture

Ideal criteria:

- active student council
- visible school events
- memes, confessions, and school-specific chatter already happen elsewhere
- no single existing anonymous school product dominates
- enough students for density
- at least one student operator willing to lead launch

### Wedge B: University micro-community, not “all students in Estonia”

Good targets:

- one university faculty
- one dorm-heavy campus cluster
- one student neighborhood

Bad target:

- “all university students everywhere”

### Wedge C: One municipality or district-level region

Ideal criteria:

- strong local identity
- existing youth centre activity
- town or district page chatter already exists
- enough boredom, pride, gossip, and event talk to sustain a local feed

### Wedge D: Affluent sponsor layer

Use affluent adults as:

- private-school champions
- alumni-network amplifiers
- municipal introducers
- local business sponsors for launch prizes
- low-ego angels who want youth-community or local-tech credibility

Do not use them as the main product persona.

## Positioning By Audience

### For students

Message:

- your school’s anonymous full-hour drop
- say what people are actually thinking
- no permanent cringe archive
- local jokes, local drama, local honesty

### For regional users

Message:

- your place, not the whole internet
- local talk without real-name performance
- temporary posts, local relevance

### For school leaders and youth workers

Message:

- not a public free-for-all
- bounded communities
- backend accountability
- moderation tools
- no visible author identities for regular users
- temporary content

### For affluent introducers and sponsors

Message:

- an Estonian community product with a disciplined rollout plan
- youth engagement without ad-network sludge
- place-based digital community with moderation and accountability

## Solving Zero Network Effect

Epsu cannot wait for users to create the network.
It has to manufacture the first density ethically.

### The first 100-user system

For each pilot community:

- recruit 1 lead host
- recruit 1 backup host
- pre-commit 20-30 founding users
- seed 15-25 genuine first posts before public opening
- prepare 10 place-specific prompts
- prepare 5 controversial-but-safe prompts
- prepare 5 useful prompts
- schedule 2 launch events or moments

### The first-post framework

Good opening content categories:

- school-specific jokes
- anonymous “what nobody says out loud here”
- cafeteria, schedule, dorm, bus, class, teacher, local venue talk
- event reaction threads
- low-stakes confessions
- practical local questions

Bad opening content categories:

- brand announcements
- generic “welcome to Epsu”
- forced corporate meme templates
- posts clearly written by adults pretending to be students

### Success threshold

A pilot is alive when it reaches:

- 30+ WAU in one community
- 10+ repeat contributors
- 3+ daily sessions per active heavy user cohort
- enough new content that users expect the next drop

If it cannot hit that in 2-3 weeks with strong local help, do not scale it.
Fix or abandon it.

## Recommended 90-Day Plan

### Phase 1: Choose 3 pilot communities

Pick only 3:

- 2 school communities
- 1 pilot regional community

Prefer:

- Tallinn
- Tartu
- one smaller place with strong local identity

Do not pick the most prestigious institutions first if access will be slow.
Pick the places most likely to activate.

### Phase 2: Build the operator layer

For each pilot:

- 1 paid lead ambassador/host
- 1 volunteer or low-stipend backup
- 1 adult ally where needed

Lead host responsibilities:

- recruit first 30 users
- collect local prompts
- seed first content
- run one launch moment
- report product issues

### Phase 3: Pre-launch waitlist, but only locally

Use:

- simple landing page
- QR codes
- school-specific or town-specific signups
- one referral mechanic

Measure:

- which school converts best
- which message converts best
- who wants to host or moderate

Do not confuse waitlist size with real traction.

### Phase 4: Launch event and week-1 ritual

Each pilot should have:

- a launch day moment
- a “first drop” moment
- one weekly ritual

Examples:

- Monday confession drop
- Thursday school gripe hour
- Friday “what happened this week” drop
- Sunday local plans thread

### Phase 5: Retention before expansion

Do not open new communities until one existing one is obviously alive.

## Budget Strategy

### Low-budget stack

Spend first on:

- student/operator stipends
- local creator micro-payments
- posters, QR cards, launch materials
- small event support
- referral or reward budget
- landing page / analytics / attribution basics

Spend later on:

- paid social support for live pilots
- retargeting
- simple creator packages

Avoid early spend on:

- national influencer campaigns
- polished brand films
- large agency retainers
- big out-of-home
- broad app-install campaigns

### Suggested first 90-day budget bands

`Lean`

- 3 pilot communities
- 3 lead hosts
- 3 backup helpers
- small physical materials
- small prizes
- small paid tests

`Moderate`

- same structure plus
- 5-10 local creators
- 1 stronger paid social test per live pilot
- 1 part-time operations lead

## The “Rich People” Strategy

This should be reframed as a sponsorship and access strategy.

### Good affluent targets

- private-school leadership circles
- school founders
- education-minded business owners
- alumni with money and local pride
- local property, hospitality, retail, or service owners who want youth reach
- angel investors who care about Estonia, youth, or local community

### Their role

- fund a pilot
- introduce Epsu to a school or municipality
- sponsor launch rewards
- provide local legitimacy
- open local media doors

### Bad affluent strategy

- trying to build a luxury identity around Epsu
- marketing Epsu as a private elite club
- optimizing for wealthy users before student or regional density exists

## Experiments To Run

Run these in order:

1. `School-vs-school messaging`
Which converts better:
- “your school’s anonymous drop”
- “say what people actually think here”
- “temporary local confessions”

2. `Student-council intro vs creator intro`
Which produces more week-1 actives?

3. `Poster + QR vs IG/TikTok-only`
For each school, measure installs and first opens.

4. `Seeded-post volume`
Does 10 seeded posts work, or do you need 25?

5. `Drop cadence messaging`
Which gets more returns:
- next full-hour drop
- tonight’s drop
- tomorrow’s confessions open

6. `Regional prompt type`
- practical town posts
- local humor
- anonymous opinions

## KPIs That Matter

Track these, not vanity reach:

- installs per pilot community
- activated users per pilot community
- day-1, day-7 retention by community
- posts per 100 activated users
- replies per top-level post
- % of users who return for second drop
- moderator load per 100 posts
- referral share after density forms

For school pilots, also track:

- percentage of signups from direct peer invite
- percentage from QR/poster
- percentage from creator content
- number of distinct active student subgroups participating

## What Epsu Should Research Next

The next research questions should not be generic. They should answer operating decisions:

- Which Estonian schools have the strongest student-council and event culture?
- Which municipalities have the strongest youth-centre-led activity?
- Which school-level creators on TikTok/Instagram can move 30-100 local students?
- Which school types are more likely to accept anonymous but moderated products?
- Which town identities are strongest for regional pilots?
- What moderation and legal language will school leaders need before permitting any association?

## Bottom Line

The path that is most likely to work is:

- do not chase all of Estonia at once
- do not chase rich users as the first audience
- do not rely on paid ads to create community
- do use tightly scoped pilots
- do use local student and youth operators
- do make school-by-school and town-by-town launch kits
- do treat moderation as part of acquisition

If Epsu wins anywhere, it will first win as a dense local ritual in one place.
After that, it can spread place by place.
